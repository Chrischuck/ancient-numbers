# Arithmetic evidence audit: Americas and Pacific

Research conducted 15–16 October 2026 for the eleven profiles in `src/data/arithmetic-americas-pacific.ts`. All four operators have a concrete procedure; an evidence label states whether that procedure is documented, reconstructed, modern, or a calculation followed by notation. This is an audit of the consulted evidence, not a claim that a people could not calculate when a particular working instruction was not recovered.

The exact notation variant matters. A school text written in Cherokee language with Hindu-Arabic digits does not document arithmetic in Sequoyah’s numerical signs. Acolhua cadastral figures are not interchangeable with Mexica tribute flags. Contemporary Māori/Hawaiian decimal number words should not silently become historical pair/four-group units. A knot recording is distinguished from the movable counters that produced its total.

## Kaktovik Iñupiaq

Targeted searches: `Kaktovik numerals multiplication division graphical Unicode arithmetic`; `Making the Old Way Count multiplication`; `site.ankn.uaf.edu Kaktovik multiplication`.

Inspected the complete relevant text of [Marín Silva, Miller and Strand’s 2021 Unicode proposal](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2021/21058-kaktovik-numerals.pdf), particularly pp. 1–2, the abacus image and figures 10–12. The proposal is coauthored by classroom teacher Catherine Strand and reproduces [Bartley’s 1997 account](https://ankn.uaf.edu/sop/SOPv2i1.pdf) as figure 13. The original newsletter download timed out; its reproduction and bibliographical identification were available in the proposal.

The evidence supports graphical addition, removing strokes for subtraction, and partial-quotient division through matched divisor shapes. Four unit beads and three five-beads in each abacus column correspond to a digit’s components. These yield concrete exchanges: five units become one five; four fives become one unit of the next twenty-place. The consulted proposal does not supply a complete multiplication recipe. That lesson therefore applies modern equal-component products and regrouping rather than falsely attributing a particular multiplication table to the documented classroom history. All arithmetic is identified as contemporary, following the 1994 invention.

## Maya

Targeted searches: `Maya arithmetic addition subtraction multiplication division ancient algorithms scholars`; `Arithmetic in Maya Numerals Anderson full pdf`; `Rojo-Garibaldi Rangoni 2021 Maya`.

Inspected the [1971 Anderson article’s publisher abstract and bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-antiquity/article/abs/arithmetic-in-maya-numerals/01B4A1933FE7F1D4391872EE80B6FDC9), which establish that four-operator demonstrations exist; the full article was not accessible. Correct publication date is January 1971, despite its 2017 online date and occasional secondary citations to 1969.

Rendered and visually inspected both pages of [Ramírez Alfonsín and Testard, _The Maya and the Conception of Mixbaal_ (1995)](https://imag.umontpellier.fr/~ramirez/Article-maya.pdf). Page 9 demonstrates `100 + 1007 + 135 = 1242` by collecting dots and bars, then `925 × 407 = 376475` with a product grid and diagonal accumulation. Its reference to Sánchez’s 1961 teaching book does not make the illustration surviving ancient scratch work. The code labels the manipulation as a reconstruction. A useful original implementation detail is the dot/bar product: dot×dot gives dot; dot×bar gives bar; bar×bar gives 25, represented as a higher-row dot and a current-row bar.

[Rojo-Garibaldi et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2021.e06580) distinguish ordinary powers-of-twenty notation from the Long Count’s mixed place factors. The PMC endpoint returned a bot check; the author manuscript was discoverable through Granada’s repository and ResearchGate. None of these inspected materials established a single ancient general-purpose multiplication/division recipe for this app’s ordinary `1, 20, 400, …` rows. The four lessons nevertheless provide complete operations in those rows, with the historical qualification directly attached. They do not apply ordinary four-bar carries to a Long Count calendar date.

## Mexica / Aztec

Targeted searches: `Texcoco land arithmetic fractions perimeter area Aztec mathematics Science 2008`; `Aztec arithmetic revisited pdf`; `Acolhua algorithm average areas`; `Nahua arithmetic nepohualtzintzin evidence`; `Aztec arithmetic addition codex`.

Cross-checked the existing [Oregon Nahua Glyphs number source](https://blogs.uoregon.edu/nahuaglyphs/numbers/) with the primary research abstracts of [Williams and Jorge y Jorge (2008)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18388287/) and [Jorge et al. (2011)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3174618/). The first concerns reconstruction of field-area and congruence arithmetic; the second tests the numerical consistency and accuracy of land records. Publisher/PMC fetches were intermittently blocked, but indexed abstract and article text were available. The full 2008 paper was not available from its publisher during this audit; no uninspected detailed rule is asserted from it.

The land evidence establishes sophisticated arithmetic relationships without supplying a complete general procedure with the app’s tribute flags, feather signs and bags. Each operator is therefore reconstructed directly in those signs: join and exchange twenties; remove with downward exchanges; make equal bundles; or form divisor-sized bundles and retain a remainder. The app does not illustrate an alleged ancient bead-abacus technique on the strength of unsourced modern claims about the nepohualtzintzin. Nor does it relabel Texcocan cadastral dots as unit dots: that variant assigns its marks differently.

## Inka khipu

Targeted searches: `Inca yupana Guaman Poma Acosta maize arithmetic division scholarly reconstruction`; `Sistema de numeración Inka en la Yupana y el Khipu`; `Counting and Arithmetic of the Inca`.

Inspected [Catepillán and Szymanski (2012), _Counting and Arithmetic of the Inca_](https://www.revista.etnomatematica.org/index.php/RevLatEm/article/download/45/373/1633), printed pp. 54–64. It reproduces accounts by Acosta (1590) and Garcilaso (1609), including manipulation of maize/pebbles and accounting with knots. It explicitly compares incompatible interpretations of Guamán Poma’s board. The arithmetic examples then select a decimal counter-row interpretation, use Radicati’s proposals for the first three operations, and identify the division procedure as the authors’ own.

This supplies a concrete working model: merge kernels and exchange ten upwards; remove with exchanges downwards; use digit-pair cells and diagonal totals for multiplication; use leading digits, partial quotients, products and remainders for division. The modern multiplication proposal itself draws on a twentieth-century Maya reconstruction. These mechanics cannot be described as a secure decipherment of the historical board.

Checked [Florio’s 2025 monograph description](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-92662-4), which proposes a different additive interpretation. This is additional evidence that exact historical board operation remains contested, not grounds for denying historical arithmetic. [Scher’s khipu account](https://smarthistory.org/inka-khipu/) supports the common numerical knot conventions used to record the answer. The interaction should show a visibly labeled counter-board reconstruction with the resulting knot cord alongside it, rather than moving knots as though they were loose beads.

## Sequoyah’s Cherokee numerals

Targeted searches: `Cherokee Sequoyah arithmetic numerals manuscript multiplication tables division`; `Sequoyah numeral manuscript arithmetical table 1839 Yale numbers`; `Sequoyah arithmetic manuscript`.

Read the original-numeral discussion by numerical-notation researcher [Stephen Chrisomalis](https://thereader.mitpress.mit.edu/sequoyah-and-the-almost-forgotten-history-of-cherokee-numerals/), including its image of the Payne manuscript and its references to Lowery’s biographical evidence. The [Gilcrease collection record](https://collections.gilcrease.org/object/4026312) returned 403. The scholar’s description gives a concrete limit: surviving direct nineteenth-century evidence for these signs is exceptionally restricted. The [Cherokee Nation poster collection](https://language.cherokee.gov/posters/syllabary-and-numbers/) and [original-number chart](https://language.cherokee.gov/media/541f3lhk/sequoyah_original_numbers_updated.pdf) establish forms and compositions, not four working algorithms.

Also inspected the [1870 Cherokee-English arithmetic multiplication-page record](https://wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Elementary_arithmetic_in_Cherokee_and_English.pdf/83), reached through the MAA historical-treasure description. This book uses ordinary decimal figures; it is a useful historical finding but cannot establish a technique in Sequoyah’s original signs.

All four panels therefore explain a concrete modern counter calculation and recording with whole-value signs. The distinction is material: the forty sign means forty, not digit four in a tens column. The original-sign artwork remains bounded to verified specimens; unsupported intermediate amounts must use an explicit quantity label instead of invented signs. Lack of this particular working evidence is not a claim about Cherokee mathematical capability.

## Choctaw

Targeted searches: `Choctaw Nation math addition subtraction multiplication division language`; `site.choctawnation.com arithmetic`; `Chahta na-holhtina 1835`; `Choctaw arithmetic digitized`; `Choctaw addition language math`.

The [Nation’s October 2018 Biskinik chart](https://www.choctawnation.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/oct-2018-biskinik.pdf) and [money lesson](https://www.choctawnation.com/lessons/choctaw-2/chapter-10/lesson-2/) support the implemented number words. The number charts and sentence lessons do not establish a traditional mental procedure for all four operators. The app therefore gives full modern joining, removal, equal-set and equal-group calculations while naming quantities in these forms.

A useful historical lead was found, rather than dismissed: Choctaw Nation member and language researcher [Jacqueline Brixey’s resource list](https://www.jacqueline-brixey.com/choctaw-resources.html) links Alfred Wright’s _Chahta na-holhtina, or Choctaw arithmetic_. The [HathiTrust catalog](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/100783607) verifies its 1835 date, language and 72-page extent; the [full-view item](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=iau.31858047116003;view=1up;seq=7) was blocked with 403. The existence of this book is documented, but its uninspected pages are not cited for an invented algorithm. This limit is recorded explicitly. The later modern worked lessons remain complete.

## Diné / Navajo

Targeted searches: `Diné Navajo math addition subtraction multiplication division curriculum language`; `Navajo Area Mathematics Guidelines joining`; `site.eric.ed.gov ED164157`.

Downloaded and text-inspected the complete 363-page primary [_Navajo Area Mathematics Guidelines_](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED164157.pdf), first printing 1975 and second printing 1977. Its issuing institution is the Bureau of Indian Affairs, Navajo Area, Window Rock. It is an English-language modern curriculum specifically intended for Navajo children, rather than evidence of a pre-contact or universally shared Diné algorithm.

Concrete content establishes all four teaching procedures. Objective 42-J, PDF pp. 97–98, joins 42 and 19 with straws and ten-bundles, then records 61. Objectives 62-J and 65-J introduce repeated equal sets and tens/units multiplication; 71-J extends products to two-place factors. Objectives 81-S–83-S model separation with decimal exchanges. Objectives 87-S–89-S form equal groups, retain leftovers, then develop partial quotients. All four are labeled modern, and the separate [Diné College chart](https://warriorweb.dinecollege.edu/ICS/Portlets/ICS/Portlet.Resources/ViewHandler.ashx?id=082af43e-c530-4f62-832f-369283e05afc) supplies quantity names. Morphological connectors are not described as mandatory mental moves.

## Māori

Targeted searches: `Māori traditional arithmetic counting pairs division Best numeration`; `site.tahurangi.education.govt.nz pāngarau tāpiri tango whakarea whakawehe`.

Inspected the Ministry of Education’s [Tihei Pāngarau, Tūārere 3](https://kauwhatareo.tahurangi.education.govt.nz/mi/tihei-p-ngarau---te-tau-me-te-taurangi---t-rere-3/5637202408.p) operation descriptions and [He Kōwae Ako Pāngarau](https://tahurangi.education.govt.nz/kauwhata-reo-online/he-k-wae-ako-p-ngarau/5637208347.p?domain=kauwhatareo.tahurangi.education.govt.nz) teaching-unit list. These directly support modern number-decomposition approaches, making tens, count-on/count-back, arrays and the relation between products and quotients. They provide a stronger foundation for the selected contemporary decimal converter than inventing an ancient technique from word order.

Inspected the numeration section of [Te Koha and Masouleh, _Ngā Inenga Māori_ (2019)](https://www.measurement.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/Nga-Inenga-Maori-A-Preliminary-Study-on-Maori-Measurement.pdf), pp. 11–15, and located [Best’s 1906 ethnographic account](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/periodicals/TPRSNZ1906-39.2.8.1.15). Historical paired counting means that a counted unit can be two objects; it is not positional base 2. The modern report explicitly cautions about the older terminology. None of these inspected texts supplies one historical four-operator procedure for the contemporary decimal words used by this converter. Modern concrete arithmetic is therefore identified as such, with the historical unit distinction explained beside it.

## Hawaiian

Targeted searches: `Hawaiian arithmetic traditional counting division mathematics pair kauna`; `J H Kanepuu traditional counting`; `He Helu Kamalii multiplication`.

Inspected [Kaliko Trapp’s decimal-number lesson](https://oleloonline.com/hawaiian-number-basics-1-to-100-0502v/) and [traditional-group lesson](https://oleloonline.com/0503v-traditional-hawaiian-number-groups/) alongside the Bishop Museum’s primary-source translation of [J. H. Kanepuu’s 1867 newspaper account](https://blog.bishopmuseum.org/nupepa/j-h-kanepuu-on-traditional-counting/). Kanepuu records actual market grouping and code-switching between older group counts and school decimal counts. He also refers to the group table and multiplication table in _He Helu Kamalii_ (1847).

The documented examples include one lau plus nine kaʻau equaling 760 fish, and three lau plus two kaʻau plus five kāuna equaling 1,300 taro tops. This is concrete historical use of grouped quantities, not a recovered four-operator long-calculation manual. Default panels therefore work contemporary decimal words, and explain the different exchanges when traditional objects are counted: four objects per kāuna, ten kāuna per kaʻau. The input unit and grouping context remain explicit through every operation. No modern digit algorithm is retrospectively labeled ancient Hawaiian practice.

## Mangarevan

Targeted searches: `Mangarevan invention binary steps calculation multiplication division Bender Beller`; `The Power of 2 Mangarevan mental arithmetic`.

Inspected all relevant sections and tables of [Bender and Beller’s PNAS paper (2013)](https://math.hawaii.edu/~tom/ethnomathematics/PNAS-2013.pdf), especially pp. 3–5, and the description of their [2017 follow-up cognitive analysis](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12337). The article explicitly distinguishes its conceptual arithmetic analysis from unavailable observations of historical performance. It supplies actual procedures, so a missing-evidence notice alone is inadequate.

The implementation follows the word structure’s mixed exchanges: ten units make takau, then each pair of terms makes the next group up to varu. Multiplication by 2, 4 or 8 shifts group levels; other factors combine those shifted groups. Division reverses the steps for powers of two. The authors explicitly assume a preference for certain divisors and large varu groups; this cannot be advertised as observed universal historical division. Other divisors use a transparent grouping extension with remainder. The quantity is tauga, not automatically individual objects, and the selected object-specific unit stays constant throughout a problem.

## Oksapmin

Targeted searches: `Oksapmin multiplication division body counting Saxe study`; `Saxe 2015 16 subtraction Oksapmin`.

Read Saxe’s primary [body-counting explanation](https://culturecognition.com/new-page-3) and [field-film commentary](https://culturecognition.com/counting-and-extension-to-arithmetic-1978-2014), alongside the indexed full-text figure descriptions of his [2015 study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1080/02103702.2015.1054669). The publisher fetch timed out, but the research companion gives detailed procedures in the researcher’s own account.

Addition/subtraction developed in the changing contexts of school and trade. The specific double-enumeration subtraction counts removed positions 16 through 10, matching each to a second seven-position count and leaving 9. The trade-store owner’s halved-body strategy for currency is a different procedure; it should not be conflated with that school example. Forward coordination supplies the applied addition lesson. No consulted source established the repeated-block multiplication/division algorithms as observed traditional practices, so those two lessons are explicitly teaching extensions, with completed blocks tracked separately from current position. All demos honor the app’s first-traversal bound; the 27 body positions are not presented as positional base-27 digits.

## Implementation handoff

Each profile’s `operationNotes` describes an actual action in the selected representation. Notes that report an evidential boundary also supply a transparent procedure, rather than ending the walkthrough. The selected operator should control both the prose and working board. Addition panels do not repeat a general four-operation history; multiplication may use joining only as part of its actual equal-group or partial-product method.

Visual tool panels should identify their status directly. Kaktovik can show its documented 3:4 abacus. Khipu can show a labeled decimal-kernel board reconstruction beside the answer cord. Maya multiplication can show dot/bar partial products. Spoken systems can show modern grouped counters alongside the appropriate number names. None should silently invent a historical tool for an oral number-word tradition merely to fill a visual slot.
